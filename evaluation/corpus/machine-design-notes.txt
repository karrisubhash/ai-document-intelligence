THEORY OF MACHINES - UNIT 4 LECTURE NOTES
Brakes, Clutches and Dynamometers
Department of Mechanical Engineering - course notes, not a regulation

4.1 Introduction
A brake is a device by which artificial frictional resistance is applied to a
moving machine member in order to retard or stop the motion of a machine. The
energy absorbed by the brake is dissipated as heat. The capacity of a brake
depends on the unit pressure between the braking surfaces, the coefficient of
friction and the time required to bring the wheel to rest.

4.2 Time required to stop a rotating drum
For a drum rotating at a known speed, the minimum time required to bring it
to rest is found by equating the work done by the braking torque to the
kinetic energy of the rotating parts. A typical examination problem gives the
mass, the radius of gyration and the speed, and asks for the minimum braking
torque required.

4.3 Simple block brake
In a simple block brake, a block is pressed against the rim of a revolving
drum by a lever. When the angle of contact is less than 60 degrees, the
normal force may be assumed uniform over the block. The required force at the
end of the lever depends on the direction of rotation of the drum, and the
brake is self-locking when the moment of the frictional force exceeds that of
the applied force.

4.4 Band brake
A band brake consists of a flexible steel band lined with friction material.
The ratio of the tensions on the tight and slack sides is given by the
exponential of the product of the coefficient of friction and the angle of
lap. Students should memorise this relation; it appears in some form in
nearly every end-semester paper on this unit.

4.5 Internal expanding shoe brake
Used in motor vehicles. The shoes are pressed outward against the inner
surface of the drum. The braking torque is the sum of the torques on the
leading and trailing shoes, and the leading shoe contributes the greater
share.

4.6 Dynamometers
A dynamometer measures the power output of an engine. Absorption
dynamometers, such as the prony brake and the rope brake, absorb the power
and dissipate it as heat. Transmission dynamometers, such as the belt
transmission dynamometer and the torsion dynamometer, transmit the power and
measure it on the way.

4.7 Fine adjustment and return springs
The return spring withdraws the brake shoe when the applied force is
released. A fine adjustment screw compensates for wear of the lining, so that
the clearance and the travel of the lever remain within the limits given in
the design data book kept in the department library.

4.8 Clutches
A single plate clutch transmits torque by friction between the flywheel, the
friction plate and the pressure plate. Under uniform wear, the intensity of
pressure is inversely proportional to the radius, and the torque transmitted
is less than under uniform pressure. Cone clutches require a smaller axial
force for the same torque but are sensitive to wear.

4.9 Worked example
A vehicle of mass 1200 kg moving at 72 km per hour is brought to rest in a
distance of 50 metres. Find the average braking force and the time required.
The kinetic energy is 240 kilojoules, the average braking force is 4800
newtons, and the time required is 5 seconds.
